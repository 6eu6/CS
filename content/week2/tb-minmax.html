min(), max() and sum(): best, worst and total

The question it answers

"Which one is the biggest/smallest by my rule, and what do they add up to?"

max([4, 9, 2])                    # 9
max("banana", "fig", key=len)     # 'banana'      several arguments work too
max(["banana", "fig"], key=len)   # 'banana'      or one list
min([], default=0)                # 0             avoids ValueError on empty data
sum([1.5, 2.5])                   # 4.0

The killer combo: max(d, key=d.get)

sales = {"tea": 140, "cake": 95, "coffee": 210}
best = max(sales, key=sales.get)    # 'coffee'

Looping over a dict gives its keys. key=sales.get means "judge each key by its value". The result is the key with the biggest value: the answer to "which product sold most?" in one line. Note sales.get without brackets: we pass the method itself for max to call.

key= gives you the whole record

staff = [{"name": "Sara", "sales": 31}, {"name": "Omar", "sales": 44}]
top = max(staff, key=lambda s: s["sales"])
print(top["name"], top["sales"])    # Omar 44   — the whole dict, not just 44

This replaces the whole "max pattern" loop from Week 2 when you only need the winner. Keep the loop when you need several things in one pass (max and min and count…).

Real-work scenarios

  • Dashboard KPIs: sum(order_totals), max(order_totals), sum(x) / len(x) for the average.
  • Outliers: max(readings) - min(readings) is the range; a sudden big range means a faulty sensor.
  • Biggest change either way: abs(x) is the distance from zero (abs(-16) → 16), so max(changes, key=abs) finds the largest rise or fall.
  • Longest line in a file (to size a table column): max(len(l) for l in lines).
  • Counting with sum: sum(1 for t in temps if t > 25) counts hot days; True counts as 1, so sum(t > 25 for t in temps) works too.
Traps

max([]) crashes with ValueError: use default=. max(["10", "9"]) is "9" because strings compare letter by letter: convert to numbers first. Don't name a variable max or sum: it hides the built-in for the rest of the program.
